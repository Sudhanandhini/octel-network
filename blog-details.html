7 Common Accounting Mistakes Small Businesses Make

Most accounting problems we see aren't caused by bad software — they're caused by small habits that quietly snowball over a year. Here are the seven we run into most often, and how to fix each one before it costs you.

1. Mixing personal and business expenses

It seems harmless until tax season, when every mixed transaction has to be manually untangled. A dedicated business account and card from day one saves hours of reconciliation later.

2. Not tracking receivables closely

Revenue on paper isn't cash in the bank. Businesses that don't actively chase overdue invoices routinely underestimate how much cash they're actually missing.

“Cash flow problems are rarely a revenue problem — they're a visibility problem.”

3. Treating bookkeeping as a year-end task

Waiting until tax season to reconcile a year of transactions guarantees errors and missed deductions. Monthly closes catch problems while they're still small.

  • Reconcile bank and card accounts every month, not every year
  • Categorize expenses consistently from month one
  • Keep a rolling cash-flow forecast, even a simple one
  • Review margins by product or service line quarterly

None of these fixes require expensive tools — just consistency, and ideally, a second set of eyes that catches what founders are too busy to notice.
